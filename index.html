Equity curves — 1x (unleveraged paper reference)

Unleveraged reference. Per-trade P/L is identical to 50x — leverage only changes how many positions can be open at once.

Equity curves — 50x (OANDA retail)

Same signals, OANDA's 50:1 retail default. Risk per trade is identical to 1x (a SL always costs 1% of equity). What changes is capacity — more positions can be open at once. If lines go to zero, it's because the strategy hit the OANDA 50% margin closeout rule.

50x stress test — what happens to recent strategies

Every line is a different strategy at 50x leverage. Green = profitable, red = blew the account. This is the truth about whether the loop is finding strategies that survive OANDA's intended environment.

Twin comparison — same strategy, two leverage regimes

Risk invariant: a SL hit always costs exactly RISK_PCT × equity regardless of leverage. So return / DD / Sharpe on a per-trade basis are identical between 1x and 50x. The only thing leverage changes is how many positions can be open at once (max_concurrent) and therefore the total number of trades that get filled. Higher max_concurrent at 50x means the strategy has more capacity — and a real OANDA account needs it.
